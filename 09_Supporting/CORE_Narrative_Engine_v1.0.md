# CORE Narrative Engine
## Version 1.0

### Purpose

This document defines the narrative system used to turn real business situations, audience tensions, and world-class thinking into compelling, original narratives that build the CORE worldview.

This is not a content calendar, book-summary system, or copywriting formula. It is the storytelling layer between the CORE intellectual universe and the audience.

---

# 1. Strategic Role of Narrative

CORE has substantial intellectual depth. Intellectual depth alone does not guarantee relevance.

The audience should move through:

> “Interesting.”
>
> → “That is happening in my business.”
>
> → “I had not looked at it that way.”
>
> → “That matters.”
>
> → “I want to explore this further.”

Narrative creates that movement.

The objective is not to make sophisticated ideas simpler. It is to make sophisticated ideas relevant to situations the audience already recognizes.

---

# 2. Narrative Philosophy

### Start with reality
Begin with a situation the audience recognizes.

### Do not start with the book
Books and authors provide intellectual lenses. They are not the subject.

### Diagnose before advising
Do not tell founders what they should do before establishing what is actually happening.

### Create distinctions
Separate things that are routinely conflated:

- Signal vs opportunity
- Opportunity vs initiative
- Interest vs evidence
- Technical feasibility vs commercial viability
- Analysis vs learning
- Strategy vs plan
- Activity vs progress

### Use pain and possibility
Do not build a fear-based content machine. Use pain, gain, curiosity, aspiration and identity.

### Create a shift in perspective
The reader should discover something, not merely receive an instruction.

### Let CORE emerge
CORE should be the underlying philosophy, not the sales pitch at the end of every narrative.

# 3. Audience Reality Layer

The audience operates across **Manufacturing and Deep Tech**.

Manufacturing includes businesses of every kind: FMCG, machines, sensors, automotive, components, industrial products, equipment, materials and more.

Deep Tech includes businesses solving real-world problems through biotechnology, nanotechnology, energy, defence, aerospace, semiconductors, advanced engineering and related technologies.

Content should enter through situations they actually experience:

- Customers keep requesting customisation.
- Sales pipeline is full but conversion is weak.
- Technology has several possible applications.
- The team has too many initiatives.
- The founder remains the decision bottleneck.
- A new market looks attractive.
- Competitors are moving.
- Pilots are happening but not converting.
- Engineering is busy but commercial traction is weak.
- Everyone agrees on the strategy but interprets it differently.
- The company has many ideas but few meaningful opportunities.
- Growth has exposed organisational capability gaps.

These are entry points, not predetermined conclusions.

---

# 4. Tension & Aspiration Universe

Every situation can contain several dimensions.

## Pain
What are they trying to escape?

Examples: stagnation, fragmentation, uncertainty, wasted effort, recurring conflict, founder dependence, weak conversion, strategic drift.

## Gain
What do they want to achieve?

Examples: a new growth path, stronger customer traction, strategic focus, better opportunities, faster learning, organisational autonomy, commercial clarity.

## Curiosity
What might they be missing?

> What if the problem is not what it appears to be?

## Aspiration
What could become possible?

> What if the company could discover opportunities before competitors do?

## Identity
Who does the leader or organisation want to become?

> A company that learns from its market rather than merely reacts to it.

---

# 5. Hidden Problem

The obvious problem is rarely the most interesting part of the narrative.

Ask:

> **What might actually be happening underneath this situation?**

Example:

**Surface problem:** Customers keep asking for features.

**Hidden problem:** The company may be treating market signals as product requirements.

**Revelation:** Recurring requests may reveal an unmet problem or emerging market rather than a list of features to build.

Narrative movement:

> Symptom → Interpretation → Hidden problem → New way of seeing

# 6. Intellectual Arsenal

World-class books and authors are **angles of attack**, not content topics.

The question is:

> **What can this thinker help us see differently about this business situation?**

Potential territories include:

### Critical Thinking
Daniel Kahneman, Julia Galef, Carl Sagan, Philip Tetlock

### Strategy
Richard Rumelt, Roger Martin, A.G. Lafley

### Uncertainty
Rita McGrath, Nassim Nicholas Taleb, Philip Tetlock

### Experimentation
David Bland, Alexander Osterwalder, Jeanne Liedtka

### Innovation
Clayton Christensen, Jeanne Liedtka

### Systems
Donella Meadows, Peter Senge

### Foresight
Amy Webb and related strategic-foresight thinkers

The list is an intellectual research universe, not a list of people to imitate.

---

# 7. Angle of Attack

Each source gives us a way to challenge the obvious interpretation.

**Kahneman:** Are we mistaking a coherent story for evidence?

**Galef:** Are we trying to prove that we are right instead of discovering what is true?

**Rumelt:** Are we calling a list of initiatives a strategy?

**McGrath:** Are we treating uncertainty as something to eliminate rather than something to manage?

**Bland / Osterwalder:** What assumption actually needs to be tested?

**Meadows:** Is the problem located where we are trying to fix it?

The author provides the lens. The narrative provides the application.

# 8. Narrative Personality Stack

## Seth Godin — Meaning & Worldview
Question:

> **What is the bigger idea here?**

Role: identify the larger tension, connect the observation to a broader business truth, create memorable worldview.

## Donald Miller — Relevance
Question:

> **Why should this person care?**

Role: begin from the audience's world and connect sophisticated thinking to lived experience.

The objective is not to make content simplistic. It is to reduce the distance between the idea and the reader.

## Robert McKee — Narrative Craft
Question:

> **Where is the tension, turn, contrast and change?**

Role: create movement, introduce tension, reveal a turn, create surprise, make the idea felt rather than merely explained.

## Senior Strategy Partner — Executive Diagnosis
Think in the voice and discipline of a senior McKinsey / BCG / Bain / Big Four strategy partner. This is not imitation of a firm; it is an editorial standard.

### Core characteristics

**Observation before opinion**

> “A recurring pattern we see in growing businesses is…”

**Diagnose rather than advise**

> “The issue is often not a shortage of market information. It is that the organisation has not identified which assumptions actually determine whether the opportunity is attractive.”

**Create distinctions**

> A customer request is not necessarily a customer need.
>
> A customer need is not necessarily a market opportunity.
>
> A market opportunity is not necessarily a growth opportunity for your company.

**Make implications explicit**

> If the same problem appears across multiple customers, the signal becomes more interesting. Recurrence alone, however, does not establish attractiveness.

**Do not overclaim**

Prefer:

> “This gives leadership a better basis for deciding whether the opportunity deserves further investigation.”

over:

> “This will transform your business.”

**End with a strategic question**

> “What would have to be true for this opportunity to deserve the next step?”

The authority comes from clarity of diagnosis, not from sounding authoritative.

# 10. Narrative Arc

A strong narrative creates movement.

1. **Recognition** — “I have seen this.”
2. **Tension** — “Something does not quite make sense.”
3. **Disruption** — “Maybe I have been interpreting it incorrectly.”
4. **Revelation** — “That may be what is actually happening.”
5. **Possibility** — “There may be another way to see this.”
6. **Strategic Reflection** — “What does this mean for my business?”

The reader should arrive at the conclusion rather than be pushed toward it.

---

# 11. Pain Narratives

Example:

**Situation:** Customers keep requesting custom features.

**Pain:** Engineering is becoming overloaded.

**Hidden problem:** Every request is being treated as an isolated requirement.

**Intellectual lens:** Systems thinking / customer discovery.

**Revelation:** The requests may be different expressions of the same unmet need.

**Possibility:** The feature backlog may contain a market signal.

**CORE principle:**

> **A customer request is sometimes a requirement. Sometimes it is a signal.**

---

# 12. Gain Narratives

Not every narrative needs a problem to begin with.

Example:

> **What if your customers were telling you where your next market was before you had identified it?**

Customer requests → recurring pattern → underlying problem → underserved segment → opportunity hypothesis.

Emotional movement:

> **Curiosity → Discovery → Possibility**

---

# 13. Identity Narratives

Some of the strongest narratives concern who the leader or organisation is becoming.

Example:

> **The goal is not to build a company where the founder makes better decisions.**
>
> **It is to build a company that makes better decisions without needing the founder in every room.**

This moves the narrative from strategy to organisational maturity and leadership identity.

---

# 14. Narrative Types

- **The Contrarian:** Everyone thinks X. But the real issue may be Y.
- **The Reframe:** You are calling this X. It may actually be Y.
- **The Hidden Signal:** Something everyone is ignoring may be telling you something important.
- **The Founder Moment:** A recognisable situation inside a founder's day.
- **The Thought Experiment:** What would happen if...?
- **The Case:** Real situation → tension → discovery → outcome.
- **The Teardown:** Take a familiar business situation apart.
- **The Paradox:** More opportunities can create less growth.
- **The Observation:** Something repeatedly observed in real businesses.
- **The Possibility:** Imagine if...
- **The Strategic Question:** A question that changes how the audience sees the situation.

# 15. Executive Diagnostic Standard

Before publishing, ask:

### Observation
Is this grounded in a real business phenomenon?

### Diagnosis
Have we distinguished the symptom from the underlying issue?

### Precision
Have we separated concepts that are routinely conflated?

### Evidence
Is the intellectual lens genuinely useful rather than decorative?

### Relevance
Will the intended reader recognize the situation?

### Implication
Does the insight change how the reader might interpret the situation?

### Restraint
Are we avoiding unsupported claims and unnecessary prescriptions?

### Strategic consequence
Does the reader understand why the distinction matters?

### Question
Does the ending open thought rather than demand engagement?

This is the **senior-partner editorial filter**.

# 16. World-Class Standard

The objective is not to sound sophisticated.

The objective is to produce work that is:

- intellectually rigorous
- commercially relevant
- original in synthesis
- clear in diagnosis
- grounded in real situations
- memorable without being theatrical
- useful without becoming instructional
- ambitious without overclaiming

A world-class narrative should make the reader think:

> **“I have experienced this, but I have never quite framed it this way.”**

# 17. Anti-Patterns

Avoid becoming:

**The Book Summarizer**

> “5 lessons from Kahneman.”

**The Framework Lecturer**

> “Here is my 7-step framework.”

**The Generic Motivator**

> “Successful leaders always…”

**The Fear Merchant**

> “Your business is dying because…”

**The Consultant Lecturer**

> “You need to do these five things.”

**The AI Content Machine**

Generic insight + generic hook + generic CTA.

**The CORE Salesman**

Every post ending with:

> “That is why you need CORE.”

CORE should be experienced through the thinking, not repeatedly advertised.

# 18. CORE Signature Principles

Over time, narratives should repeatedly surface ideas such as:

> **A signal is not an opportunity.**

> **Interest is not evidence.**

> **Technical feasibility is not commercial viability.**

> **More analysis does not necessarily create more certainty.**

> **An opportunity is a hypothesis about future value.**

> **The question is not whether an idea is good. It is what would have to be true for it to work.**

> **Evidence should change what you do, not merely support what you already decided.**

These are not slogans to insert mechanically. They are recurring ideas that should become associated with the CORE worldview.

# 19. Research-to-Narrative Workflow

1. **Find a real business situation.**
2. **Map pain, gain, curiosity and identity.**
3. **Identify the hidden problem.**
4. **Search the intellectual library.**
5. **Select one angle of attack.**
6. **Extract the useful idea.**
7. **Apply it to the business situation.**
8. **Find the revelation.**
9. **State the strategic implication.**
10. **Derive the CORE point of view.**
11. **Write the narrative using tension, contrast and movement.**
12. **Remove unnecessary explanation.**
13. **End with a strategic question.**

# 20. Master Content Equation

> **REALITY + TENSION + WORLD-CLASS THINKING + REVELATION + POSSIBILITY + EXECUTIVE DIAGNOSIS + YOUR POINT OF VIEW = CORE NARRATIVE**

> **The audience should recognize the problem before they recognize the theory.**
