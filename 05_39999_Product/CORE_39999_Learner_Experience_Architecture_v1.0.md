# 1. Purpose of This Document

This document defines the **learner experience architecture** for the ₹39,999 CORE product.

It is not:

- a sales page;
- a marketing narrative;
- a video script;
- a Graphy implementation manual;
- a generic course curriculum.

It defines the **experience the learner moves through** to convert a real idea/problem into a defensible market decision and next commitment.

The product is hybrid:

> **online learning + structured work + personalised sessions + evidence generation + decision-making.**

The learner should not finish with merely:

> “I learned a framework.”

They should finish with:

> **a worked strategic decision about a real idea/problem, supported by an appropriate evidence plan and a concrete next commitment.**

---

# 2. Product Intention

The ₹39,999 product is intended to:

> **Bring an idea to market.**

The transformation is therefore not:

> knowledge acquisition.

It is:

> **structured movement from an uncertain idea/problem toward a market-facing decision.**

---

# 3. Product Boundary

The ₹39,999 product is **not** intended to build the entire organisation required to scale the product.

That belongs to the higher-value strategic/system engagement.

The ₹39,999 product should help the learner:

- frame the opportunity/problem;
- make the strategic choice clearer;
- identify what must be true;
- isolate the critical uncertainty;
- determine what evidence is required;
- select an appropriate evidence-generation method;
- generate/interpret evidence;
- make a decision;
- define the next commitment.

---

# 4. Core Transformation

The learner begins with:

> **“I have an idea/problem/opportunity, but I don't know exactly what to do next.”**

The learner should finish with:

> **“I know what decision I am making, what uncertainty matters, what evidence I need, what I learned, what decision I have made, and what I will do next.”**

# 6. The Four Evidence Contexts

The four teaching cases demonstrate that the method changes according to the problem.

| Case | Context | Primary learning |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Indian startup / new product | Commercial / opportunity uncertainty |
| 02 | Existing customer problem | Diagnostic / causal uncertainty |
| 03 | Technology problem | Technical / operational uncertainty |
| 04 | Policy / regulatory problem | External-system uncertainty |

The cases are **teaching simulations**.

The learner's own problem remains the **primary working case**.

---

# 7. Design Principle

## The learner should learn through a repeating loop:

> **Learn → Apply → Submit → Discuss → Revise → Continue**

Not:

> Watch → Watch → Watch → Finish.

The personalised sessions exist to create judgment, correction and application.

---

# 8. Five-Session Architecture

The product uses five personalised online sessions.

Each session is supported by:

- pre-session content;
- learner work;
- an artifact;
- a personalised session;
- revision;
- a gate to the next stage.

---

# SESSION 1
# FRAME THE PROBLEM / OPPORTUNITY

## Objective

Move from:

> **“I have an idea/problem.”**

to:

> **“I can articulate the problem/opportunity and the decision I am trying to make.”**

---

## Learner Starting Point

The learner brings one real:

- idea;
- product;
- customer problem;
- technology;
- opportunity;
- strategic situation.

---

## Content

### Concept 1 — Situation vs Problem

Separate:

> what is happening

from:

> what we think is causing it.

### Concept 2 — Problem Frame

Understand that different problem frames lead to different strategic choices.

### Concept 3 — Opportunity

Move beyond:

> “I have a product.”

toward:

> “What opportunity/problem is worth pursuing?”

### Concept 4 — Decision

A strategic process must eventually inform a decision.

---

## Teaching Case

Use **Case 01** initially because it provides an accessible entry into:

> idea → problem → opportunity → decision.

Do not reveal the complete answer immediately.

Ask the learner:

> “What would you need to know before deciding this is worth pursuing?”

---

## Learner Task

Complete:

### Problem

`____________________________`

### Situation

`____________________________`

### Opportunity

`____________________________`

### Decision

`____________________________`

### Alternative frames

`____________________________`

---

## Artifact

### **CORE Problem & Opportunity Frame**

The learner submits the first version.

---

## Live Session

The session focuses on:

- challenging the problem frame;
- separating symptoms from causes;
- clarifying the opportunity;
- making the decision explicit;
- identifying ambiguity.

The coach should **not solve the problem for the learner**.

The coach should improve the learner's reasoning.

---

## Exit Criterion

The learner can clearly state:

> **What is happening?**

> **What problem/opportunity are we pursuing?**

> **What decision are we trying to make?**

---

# SESSION 2
# STRATEGIC CHOICE + WWHTBT

## Objective

Move from:

> **“This is the opportunity.”**

to:

> **“For this choice to succeed, these conditions would have to be true.”**

---

## Content

### Concept 1 — Strategic Choice

Clarify what the learner is choosing to pursue.

### Concept 2 — WWHTBT

Identify the conditions that would have to be true for the choice to succeed.

### Concept 3 — Assumptions

Separate:

- known;
- indicated;
- assumed;
- unknown.

### Concept 4 — Fragility

Not every assumption deserves equal attention.

Ask:

> **If this condition is false, how badly does the strategy break?**

---

## Teaching Cases

Use short excerpts from:

- Case 01 — commercial uncertainty;
- Case 02 — diagnostic uncertainty;
- Case 03 — technical uncertainty.

The learner should see that WWHTBT conditions vary by situation.

---

## Learner Task

Create a:

### **WWHTBT Map**

Possible dimensions:

- customer;
- problem;
- behaviour;
- buying;
- economics;
- technology;
- integration;
- capability;
- ecosystem;
- regulation/policy.

Not every dimension must be used.

The learner chooses what matters.

---

## Artifact

### **CORE WWHTBT Map**

---

## Live Session

Challenge:

- vague assumptions;
- unnecessary assumptions;
- hidden dependencies;
- conditions that are not actually decision-critical.

The key question:

> **“Which of these, if false, would materially change your decision?”**

---

## Exit Criterion

The learner has:

- a clear strategic choice;
- a set of consequential WWHTBT conditions;
- a first ranking of uncertainty.

---

# SESSION 3
# CRITICAL UNCERTAINTY + EVIDENCE SELECTION

## Objective

This is the central evidence-design session.

Move from:

> **“We have many assumptions.”**

to:

> **“This is the uncertainty that matters most right now, and this is the evidence we need.”**

---

## Content

### Concept 1 — Not All Unknowns Matter Equally

Prioritize by:

- consequence;
- decision relevance;
- uncertainty;
- learning value;
- time sensitivity.

### Concept 2 — Existing Evidence First

Before generating new evidence:

> **What do we already know?**

### Concept 3 — Evidence Required

Describe the evidence needed **before choosing a test**.

### Concept 4 — Method Selection

Only now consult the:

> **CORE Evidence-Generation Method Library**

---

## Teaching Cases

This is where the four cases are most valuable.

Show:

### Case 01

Commercial commitment evidence.

### Case 02

Internal data + customer evidence + operational triangulation.

### Case 03

Technical test + acceptance criteria.

### Case 04

Authoritative regulatory evidence + expert/customer/commercial evidence.

Then ask:

> **“Why is there no universal CORE test?”**

---

## Learner Task

Complete the:

# **CORE Evidence Selection Card™**

Required fields:

1. Decision
2. Critical uncertainty
3. Existing evidence
4. Evidence required
5. Evidence source
6. Candidate methods
7. Selected method
8. Method limitations
9. Acceptance criteria — where applicable
10. Evidence standard
11. Decision rule
12. Stop condition

---

## Artifact

### **CORE Evidence Selection Card™**

This is a signature product asset.

---

## Live Session

The coach reviews:

- whether the critical uncertainty is actually critical;
- whether existing evidence was overlooked;
- whether the evidence requirement is correctly framed;
- whether the selected method follows from the question;
- whether the method has been overclaimed;
- whether the evidence standard matches the decision.

---

## Exit Criterion

The learner can answer:

> **What do I need to know?**

> **What evidence would reduce that uncertainty?**

> **Why is this the appropriate method?**

> **What will this method not tell me?**

---

# SESSION 4
# GENERATE / INTERPRET EVIDENCE

## Objective

Move from:

> **“I know what to test.”**

to:

> **“I have generated and interpreted evidence without overstating what it proves.”**

---

## Content

### Concept 1 — Evidence Generation

Execute the selected method.

### Concept 2 — Observation vs Interpretation

Separate:

> what happened

from:

> what we think it means.

### Concept 3 — Evidence Quality

Assess:

- relevance;
- directness;
- credibility;
- behavioural proximity;
- limitations;
- consistency.

### Concept 4 — Evidence Standard

Ask:

> **Is this evidence strong enough for the decision?**

### Concept 5 — Learning

Convert evidence into:

> what changed in our understanding?

---

## Teaching Cases

Use:

- Case 02 to demonstrate conflicting explanations;
- Case 03 to demonstrate technical evidence and acceptance criteria;
- Case 04 to demonstrate multiple evidence sources.

---

## Learner Task

Execute the selected evidence-generation activity.

Record:

- observations;
- behaviour;
- statements;
- commitments;
- contradictions;
- unexpected findings;
- limitations.

Then complete:

### **CORE Learning Card**

> We believed...

> We observed...

> We learned...

> This changed...

> What remains uncertain...

---

## Artifact

### **Evidence Record + Learning Card**

---

## Live Session

The coach challenges:

- confirmation bias;
- overinterpretation;
- weak evidence;
- unsupported causal claims;
- premature validation claims.

The central question:

> **“What does the evidence actually establish?”**

Then:

> **“What does it not establish?”**

---

## Exit Criterion

The learner can make a disciplined interpretation of the evidence.

---

# SESSION 5
# DECIDE + BUILD THE NEXT COMMITMENT

## Objective

Move from:

> **“We learned something.”**

to:

> **“We have made a decision and know what happens next.”**

---

## Content

### Concept 1 — Decision Rules

Use pre-defined logic where possible:

- GO;
- MODIFY;
- STOP;
- TEST AGAIN;
- ESCALATE;
- DEFER.

### Concept 2 — Decision

The output of evidence work is a decision, not a report.

### Concept 3 — Next Commitment

A decision should create an action.

### Concept 4 — Evidence Continues

A decision may create the next uncertainty.

Therefore:

> **Decision → Next Question → Next Commitment**

---

## Teaching Case

Use Case 04 as a final synthesis because it demonstrates:

- multiple evidence types;
- ambiguity;
- interpretation;
- strategic choice;
- next commitment.

---

## Learner Task

Produce the:

# **CORE Strategic Decision Brief**

It should contain:

1. Problem / opportunity
2. Strategic choice
3. Critical uncertainty
4. WWHTBT
5. Evidence required
6. Evidence generated
7. What the evidence revealed
8. What it does not establish
9. Decision
10. Next commitment
11. 30/60/90 path

---

## Artifact

### **CORE Strategic Decision Brief**

---

## Live Session

The final session is not primarily a teaching session.

It is a:

> **decision review.**

The coach challenges the learner to defend:

- the problem;
- the choice;
- the uncertainty;
- the evidence;
- the interpretation;
- the decision;
- the next commitment.

---

## Exit Criterion

The learner can independently articulate:

> **“This is the opportunity/problem.”**

> **“This is the choice.”**

> **“This was the critical uncertainty.”**

> **“This is the evidence we generated.”**

> **“This is what it means.”**

> **“This is our decision.”**

> **“This is what we will do next.”**

# 10. Content Architecture

Every content unit should contain five elements.

## 1. CONCEPT

What does the learner need to understand?

## 2. CASE

How does it work in a realistic situation?

## 3. WORK

What must the learner do?

## 4. ARTIFACT

What tangible output should result?

## 5. COACHING

Where does personalised intervention improve the work?

This creates:

> **Concept → Application → Output → Correction**

# 13. Personalised Session Purpose

The sessions should **not** become private lectures.

Each meeting has a specific function.

| Session | Coach role |
|---|---|
| 1 | Diagnose / challenge framing |
| 2 | Challenge strategic choice + WWHTBT |
| 3 | Challenge uncertainty + evidence selection |
| 4 | Challenge evidence + interpretation |
| 5 | Challenge decision + next commitment |

The learner should do most of the intellectual work.

The coach creates:

> **pressure, clarity, correction and judgment.**

---

# 14. Case Usage Architecture

Cases are not separate modules that the learner must “finish.”

They are used as:

> **mirrors for the learner's own situation.**

### Case 01

Use to teach:

> commercial uncertainty.

### Case 02

Use to teach:

> diagnostic uncertainty.

### Case 03

Use to teach:

> technical uncertainty.

### Case 04

Use to teach:

> regulatory/external-system uncertainty.

Then return to:

> **“What kind of uncertainty do YOU have?”**

---

# 15. Learner's Own Case Is the Main Case

This is a critical product principle.

The learner should not finish knowing how the four fictional companies solved their problems.

They should finish having worked through:

> **their own idea/problem.**

The four cases exist to develop judgment.

---

# 16. Evidence-Generation Flexibility

The product must never promise:

> “You will run X tests.”

Instead:

> **“You will learn to determine what evidence your situation deserves.”**

Depending on the learner's case, the selected method might involve:

- existing data;
- secondary research;
- expert evidence;
- customer conversations;
- user research;
- observation;
- behavioural/commitment evidence;
- prototype;
- technical validation;
- field test;
- pilot;
- controlled experiment;
- commercial test;
- model/scenario analysis;
- regulatory research;
- combinations of methods.

The method library is consulted only after the evidence requirement is established.

---

# 17. Product Gates

The learner should not progress merely because a video has been watched.

## Gate 1

Problem/opportunity is sufficiently clear.

## Gate 2

Strategic choice and WWHTBT are sufficiently clear.

## Gate 3

Critical uncertainty and evidence requirement are sufficiently clear.

## Gate 4

Evidence has been generated and interpreted with appropriate discipline.

## Gate 5

Decision and next commitment are defensible.

These are **quality gates**, not bureaucratic hurdles.

---

# 18. What the Coach Should Look For

## Session 1

Is the learner solving the right problem?

## Session 2

Is the strategic choice explicit?

## Session 3

Are they testing what matters rather than what is easy?

## Session 4

Are they overstating the evidence?

## Session 5

Are they actually making a decision?

---

# 19. What the Learner Should NOT Be Taught

Avoid turning CORE into:

- a fixed test menu;
- a generic market-research course;
- a customer-interview course;
- an experimentation course;
- a product-management course;
- a business-plan course;
- a pitch-deck course.

Those may appear as supporting techniques.

They are not the central capability.

The central capability is:

> **structured strategic judgment under uncertainty.**
