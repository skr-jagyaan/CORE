# 1. Product Definition

CORE™ Essentials is a compact learning-and-application product built around the **Business Thinking Canvas™** and **CORE™ GPT**.

Its job is not to teach:

- AI for its own sake
- a generic canvas
- presentation skills
- generic entrepreneurship

Its job is to build one practical capability:

> **Turn a business signal into a structured opportunity, think it through clearly, communicate it credibly, and prepare the smallest meaningful test.**

The product therefore moves:

> **Signal → Thinking → Opportunity → Canvas → Narrative → Stakeholder Buy-In → Test**

The product stops immediately before full market validation.

# Module 2 — The Business Thinking Canvas™

## Lesson 2.1 — What the Canvas Is

**Core idea**

The Business Thinking Canvas™ is a visual structure for turning an ambiguous business situation into:

> **a clearer opportunity + a possible next move**

It does not predict success.

It creates inspectable reasoning.

---

## Lesson 2.2 — Block 1: Strategic Gap

### Question

> **What is changing?**

Identify the gap between:

> **what is**

and

> **what could be**

**Application**

Complete Strategic Gap for the learner's signal.

**Artifact**

### Canvas v1 — Strategic Gap

---

## Lesson 2.3 — Block 2: Strategy-In-Use

### Questions

> What are we doing today?

> What assumptions are embedded in the current way of operating?

> What are we treating as true?

**Application**

Complete the existing strategy and its assumptions.

---

## Lesson 2.4 — Block 3: Alternatives

### Question

> **What else could we do?**

Generate multiple possibilities.

The goal is to prevent premature attachment to the first idea.

**Application**

Generate at least three plausible alternatives to the learner's first interpretation.

---

## Lesson 2.5 — Block 4: Better Choice

### Question

> **Which alternative deserves deeper examination?**

Important distinction:

> **Better choice ≠ guaranteed choice**

It is the option that, given current knowledge, deserves deeper examination.

---

## Lesson 2.6 — Block 5: Logic / What Would Have to Be True

### Question

> **What must be true for this choice to work?**

This is the bridge from:

> **compelling idea**

to:

> **testable opportunity**

**Application**

List the conditions that must hold.

---

## Lesson 2.7 — Block 6: Test

### Questions

> What evidence would increase confidence?

> What evidence would decrease confidence?

> What is the smallest useful way to learn?

**Application**

Draft the first meaningful test.

---

## Lesson 2.8 — Block 7: Commit

### Question

> **What deserves the next move?**

The next commitment can be:

- research
- customer observation
- conversation
- prototype
- pretotype
- small experiment
- commercial test

The commitment is not necessarily a large investment.

# Module 6 — Why an Idea Is Not Enough

## Lesson 6.1 — The Communication Problem

Most opportunity conversations begin:

> **“I have an idea.”**

The better structure is:

> **Here is the situation.**
>
> **Here is what changed.**
>
> **Here is the opportunity.**
>
> **Here is why it matters.**
>
> **Here is what we believe.**
>
> **Here is what we still do not know.**
>
> **Here is what we propose to test.**

The stakeholder is now evaluating:

> **a reasoned opportunity + controlled next step**

rather than merely an idea.

# Module 7 — The Six-Book Narrative Framework

This uses the existing CORE narrative architecture.

## 7.1 Murphy's Law

### Question

> **What happens if we do nothing or commit blindly?**

Identify the cost of:

- inaction
- delay
- missing the opportunity
- existing investment becoming less valuable
- committing before learning

---

## 7.2 Breakthrough Advertising

### Question

> **What better future could become possible?**

Connect the opportunity to:

- revenue
- margin
- market access
- customer retention
- strategic position
- capability
- speed
- risk reduction

---

## 7.3 Breakthrough + CORE Mechanism

### Structure

> **Situation → Opportunity → Mechanism → Expected Outcome**

Explain:

> **Why this opportunity creates the possibility of this outcome.**

---

## 7.4 Ogilvy

### Question

> **Why should anyone believe this?**

Use:

- observations
- customer evidence
- existing facts
- strategic logic
- case evidence
- explicit assumptions
- known constraints
- What Would Have to Be True

Then acknowledge uncertainty.

---

## 7.5 Wizard of Ads

### Question

> **What kind of opportunity is this?**

Possible categories:

- new revenue
- market entry
- capability
- adjacency
- customer value
- strategic hedge
- new business model

The goal is category clarity, not clever branding.

---

## 7.6 Kennedy

### Question

> **What is the smallest meaningful next action?**

Do not ask:

> “Do you approve the whole strategy?”

Ask for:

> **the smallest next action required to test whether this deserves further investment.**

Examples:

- customer access
- data access
- small research budget
- two-week experiment
- prototype
- pilot
- one internal resource
- one customer meeting
