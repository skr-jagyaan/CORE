# CORE™ CONTENT + PRE-SOLD ASSET OPERATING SYSTEM
## Version 1.0

## Purpose

This OS turns CORE's intellectual work into a compounding public system that builds recognition, belief, trust and readiness before a sales conversation.

It governs the creation, packaging, propagation and routing of content and pre-sold assets.

The system is not designed to maximise content volume.

It is designed to make CORE's thinking:

> visible → understandable → memorable → useful → applicable → trusted → commercially actionable

# 4. CONTENT CONSTRUCTION ENGINE

## Step 1 — Identify the audience state

Signal, Idea, Market/Product, or Organisation.

## Step 2 — Identify the tension

What is the audience currently believing, doing or struggling with?

## Step 3 — Define the belief shift

What should they believe after consuming the asset?

## Step 4 — State the non-obvious idea

The core idea must be worth the audience's attention.

## Step 5 — Provide mechanism

Explain why the observation occurs.

## Step 6 — Provide evidence

Use cases, research, actual observations, results or demonstrations.

## Step 7 — Make it memorable

Use contrast, analogy, story, language, visual structure or a strong principle.

## Step 8 — Make it actionable

Give a concrete next move without giving away the entire paid system.

## Step 9 — Route to the next asset

Each major asset should make the next step obvious.

---

# 7. SIX VALUE-CONTEXT LEVERS

The same underlying intellectual value can feel more valuable depending on context.

Use six levers deliberately:

## PLACE
Where is the idea encountered?

## MOMENT
When is it especially relevant?

## PACKAGING
How is it bundled and framed?

## STORY
What meaning surrounds it?

## ACCESS
What level of depth, participation or exclusivity is available?

## STATUS
What does using or being associated with it signal?

These levers increase perceived value without manufacturing false scarcity or exaggerated claims.

---

# 8. BEHAVIOURAL DESIGN

Use behavioural effects as secondary optimisation layers:

- halo effect;
- framing effect;
- anchoring effect;
- decoy effect;
- scarcity effect;
- social proof;
- loss-leader logic where appropriate.

Rules:

1. Strong intellectual value comes first.
2. Scarcity must be real.
3. Social proof must be relevant.
4. Framing should clarify rather than deceive.
5. Behavioural design cannot compensate for weak substance.

# 13. PRE-SOLD READINESS TEST

A prospect is increasingly pre-sold when they:

- recognise the problem;
- accept the reframing;
- believe the method is credible;
- see it demonstrated;
- can diagnose their own situation;
- understand the value of deeper access;
- trust the evidence and proof;
- know the appropriate next step.

# 15. CONTENT SCORECARD

Evaluate every major asset on:

1. Is the idea non-obvious?
2. Is the mechanism clear?
3. Is evidence credible?
4. Is the belief shift explicit?
5. Is the asset useful by itself?
6. Does it demonstrate CORE's method?
7. Is the audience state clear?
8. Does it route naturally to the next asset?
9. Can it generate future derivative assets?
10. Does it create proprietary market intelligence through response?
