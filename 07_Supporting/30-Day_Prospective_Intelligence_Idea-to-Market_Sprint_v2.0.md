## Version 2.0

## Core Positioning

> **I help leaders in Manufacturing and Deep Tech take a meaningful idea into the market, test what must be true, learn from real evidence, and decide what deserves the next commitment.**

The purpose of the 30-day sprint is not to teach AI for its own sake.

It is to help a leader move a **focal idea** from internal belief into real-market contact, evidence, learning and decision — while installing a repeatable opportunity discovery and validation system for what comes next.

The AI Personal OS is the enabling mechanism.

The primary commercial outcome is a **meaningful focal idea moved toward market reality with an evidence-backed next decision**.

The opportunity pipeline is the repeatability capability installed alongside the focal idea.

---

# The Core Promise

## Bring the idea to market — and build the capability for what comes next.

The client leaves the sprint with a system that helps them:

1. **Define** the focal idea and the market problem it is intended to solve.
2. **Identify** what would have to be true for the idea to work.
3. **Find** the critical and fragile assumptions most capable of changing the decision.
4. **Test** the critical barrier with the smallest credible market experiment.
5. **Generate evidence** from real customers, buyers, users, experts, or operating conditions.
6. **Learn** from what the market actually does rather than what people say they might do.
7. **Decide** whether to proceed, modify, stop, or test again.
8. **Maintain** a repeatable opportunity pipeline so future choices do not start from zero.

The desired state is:

> **A meaningful focal idea has been moved into real-market evidence and a clear next decision, while a continuously replenished opportunity pipeline is available for what comes next.**

# 1. Murphy's Law — Sell the Cost of NOT Having

The enemy is not simply a lack of ideas.

The enemy is **having no reliable system for discovering and qualifying opportunities before resources are committed**.

Without such a system, advanced-industry leaders can experience:

- opportunities discovered too late
- dependence on founder intuition
- fragmented market intelligence
- internal opinion masquerading as evidence
- resources committed to weak initiatives
- excessive development before market validation
- missed opportunities caused by organizational inertia
- too few strategic alternatives
- large investments made before critical assumptions are tested
- learning that disappears after individual projects

The cost is not just one bad decision.

It is the **cumulative opportunity cost of not having a continuous pipeline of better choices**.

The sprint therefore creates an institutional capability:

> **A systematic way to discover and test opportunities before investing heavily in them.**

# WEEK 3 — TEST
### Turn the Focal Opportunity into Evidence

The objective is to test the focal opportunity’s most consequential and least-proven assumptions with real-world evidence. Secondary opportunities may remain in discovery or be tested where the evidence warrants it.

---

## Day 15 — Identify the Critical and Fragile Assumption

For the focal opportunity ask:

> **What would have to be true for this idea to work?**

Then identify the assumption that is both:
- critical to the decision, and
- insufficiently supported by evidence.

Find the barrier where being wrong would most materially change the decision.

### Output

**WWHTBT Map**

---

## Day 16 — Design the Guerrilla Test

Translate the key assumption into an XYZ hypothesis:

> **At least X% of Y will Z.**

Then define:

- Biggest Unknown
- XYZ Hypothesis
- Smallest Test
- Target Audience
- Standard of Proof
- Test Duration
- Data to Capture
- Stop Conditions

### Output

**Guerrilla Test Card**

---

## Days 17–19 — Run the Test

Use the smallest experiment capable of generating meaningful evidence.

Potential formats:

- Fake Door
- Landing Page
- Direct Outreach
- Concierge Service
- Manual Facade
- Mock Sale
- Prototype Demonstration
- Pre-order
- Paid Pilot

The objective is not to build the solution.

The objective is to **test the opportunity**.

**Your Own Data**

**Evidence Set**

# WEEK 4 — DECIDE
### Convert Evidence into Investment Choices

The objective is not to validate everything.

The objective is to determine **where to invest next**.

---

## Days 22–23 — Review the Evidence

Compare:

**Hypothesis vs Evidence**

Determine:

- Was the Standard of Proof met?
- What was validated?
- What failed?
- What remains uncertain?
- What changed?
- What deserves another test?

---

# The Decision Fork

## GO

The evidence justifies further market commitment.

Move toward higher-fidelity validation, pilot, commercial development or launch preparation.

---

## MODIFY

The opportunity appears promising, but:

- segment
- offer
- value proposition
- business model
- channel

needs modification.

Return to the Opportunity Pipeline and retest.

---

## STOP

The evidence does not justify further investment.

Retire the opportunity.

Preserve the learning.

Free the resources.

---

## Days 24–27 — Indicative Test

If a Guerrilla Test passes, increase the level of commitment.

Test:

- commercial intent
- willingness to pay
- delivery feasibility
- operational viability

Possible evidence:

- Letter of Intent
- Mock Sale
- Pre-order
- Paid Pilot
- Concierge Delivery
- Commercial Commitment

The progression is:

**Interest → Behavior → Commitment → Investment**

---

## Day 28 — Rebuild the Opportunity Pipeline

The decision on one opportunity should not end the system.

The client returns to the pipeline.

Add:

- new signals
- new opportunities
- modified opportunities
- opportunities released by failed tests
- adjacent opportunities revealed through testing

This keeps the system alive.

---

## Day 29 — Make the Focal-Idea Decision

For the tested focal opportunity:

**GO / MODIFY / STOP**

For the broader portfolio:

- Which opportunities should be tested next?
- Which should be watched?
- Which should be retired?
- Where should resources be concentrated?
