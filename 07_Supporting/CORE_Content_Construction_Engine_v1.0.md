# CORE™ CONTENT CONSTRUCTION ENGINE
## Version 1.0
### Intellectual idea → belief movement → script → story → channel-native asset

---

## 1. PURPOSE

The CORE Content Construction Engine is the decision layer between the CORE intellectual system and the final content asset.

It answers:

> **Given an important idea, how do we construct the strongest piece of content for a specific ICP, audience state and channel?**

This is not a content calendar and not a prompt library.

It is a **content-construction system**.

# 4. THE SEVEN INPUTS

Every major asset begins with seven inputs.

### 1. ICP

Who is this for?

Examples:

- Founder
- CEO / MD
- Manufacturing leader
- Deep-Tech leader
- Product leader
- Technology leader
- Strategic operator

### 2. Audience State

Use the CORE state architecture:

**SIGNAL**

> What might this mean?

**IDEA**

> Can we bring this idea to market?

**MARKET / PRODUCT**

> How do we build the business around this?

**ORGANISATION**

> What organisation/system does this opportunity deserve?

### 3. Audience Tension

What is happening in their world?

Examples:

- customer interest without purchase;
- technology without adoption;
- market growth without strategic fit;
- product idea without evidence;
- many customer requests without clarity;
- successful experiments without a clear decision.

### 4. Current Belief

What might they currently believe?

Example:

> “If customers say they want it, we have validated demand.”

### 5. Desired Belief

What should they understand after consuming the asset?

Example:

> “Customer enthusiasm is a signal; commercial commitment requires stronger evidence.”

### 6. CORE Intellectual Idea

What is the actual insight?

Example:

> **A customer problem is not automatically an opportunity.**

### 7. Evidence

What makes the idea credible?

Use:

- observation;
- customer conversation;
- case;
- research;
- data;
- experiment;
- counterexample;
- demonstrated reasoning.

# 6. TACTICAL SCRIPT STRUCTURE

For short-form content, use the following default structure:

## INSIGHT #1 — FRONT-LOADED REFRAME

Introduce the primary non-obvious core premise or definition in the hook / opening lock-in zone.

The audience should immediately think:

> **“I hadn't looked at it that way.”**

Examples:

> “Your customer problem may not be the opportunity. It may only be the signal.”

> “A growing market can still be a terrible market for your company.”

Do not spend the opening on biography, generic context or “today I want to talk about…”

---

## INSIGHT #2 — THE MECHANISM

Drop the second realization in the primary body block.

It answers:

> **“So how do I actually think about this?”**

Useful mechanisms include:

- distinction;
- sequence;
- diagnostic;
- framework;
- example;
- test;
- decision rule;
- case breakdown.

---

## PAYOFF / CTA

Transition directly from:

> **mechanism → implication → payoff / CTA**

while the audience is still experiencing the cognitive reward.

Do not add a long generic conclusion.

### Golden formula

> **Surprise them → teach them → reward them → move them.**

---

# 7. INSIGHT #1 CONSTRUCTION PATTERNS

Use one of these when appropriate:

### Pattern interrupt

> Most leaders assume X. The problem is…

### Counterintuitive definition

> X isn't actually X. It is…

### Hidden distinction

> The important distinction isn't A vs B. It's…

### Reversal

> The thing you think is evidence may actually be the source of uncertainty.

### Unexpected consequence

> The more customers ask for this feature, the less certain you may be about the product you should build.

---

# 8. INSIGHT #2 CONSTRUCTION PATTERNS

Useful structures:

### Three-part distinction

> Signal → Opportunity → Evidence

### Decision sequence

> Observe → Frame → Test → Decide

### Test logic

> Uncertainty → evidence required → test → decision

### Diagnostic

> If X, examine Y before committing to Z.

### Case breakdown

> Situation → assumption → evidence → result → decision

### Comparison

> What most teams do → what CORE does differently

# 10. SEVEN STORYTELLING FRAMEWORKS

## 1. HERO'S JOURNEY

Best for:

- customer transformation;
- founder/operator journey;
- difficult strategic transition.

Structure:

```text
CUSTOMER / OPERATOR
      ↓
PROBLEM
      ↓
GUIDANCE
      ↓
TRANSFORMATION
```

CORE rule:

> The customer is the hero. CORE is the guide.

---

## 2. BEFORE–AFTER–BRIDGE

Best for:

- value explanation;
- product transformation;
- practical change.

Structure:

```text
BEFORE
Current pain
   ↓
AFTER
Desired state
   ↓
BRIDGE
What changes / how
```

---

## 3. PROBLEM–AGITATE–SOLUTION

Best for:

- problem awareness;
- costly mistakes;
- urgent pain.

Structure:

```text
PROBLEM
   ↓
CONSEQUENCE / TENSION
   ↓
SOLUTION / RELIEF
```

CORE constraint:

> Agitation must create clarity, not manipulation.

---

## 4. STORYBRAND

Best for:

- category clarity;
- positioning;
- customer journey.

Structure:

```text
CUSTOMER = HERO
PROBLEM = VILLAIN
CORE = GUIDE
PLAN = METHOD
ACTION = STEP
SUCCESS = OUTCOME
```

---

## 5. PIXAR PITCH

Best for:

- narrative cases;
- sequential business stories;
- change over time.

Structure:

```text
ONCE UPON A TIME
EVERY DAY
UNTIL ONE DAY
BECAUSE OF THAT
UNTIL FINALLY
```

---

## 6. DATA STORY

Best for:

- research;
- market intelligence;
- evidence;
- surprising findings.

Structure:

```text
CONTEXT
   ↓
SURPRISING FINDING / CONFLICT
   ↓
WHAT IT MEANS
   ↓
WHAT CHANGES
   ↓
WHAT TO DO NEXT
```

CORE principle:

> **Data without interpretation is noise. Interpretation without evidence is opinion.**

---

## 7. CONFESSION

Best for:

- personal learning;
- mistakes;
- misconceptions;
- intellectual humility.

Structure:

```text
MISTAKE / MISCONCEPTION
      ↓
WHAT I LEARNED
      ↓
WHAT CHANGED
      ↓
WHAT OTHERS CAN AVOID / TRY
```

The goal is earned credibility, not performative vulnerability.

# 11. STORY FRAME SELECTION MATRIX

| Situation | Preferred framework |
|---|---|
| Customer transformation | Hero's Journey |
| Product/value explanation | Before–After–Bridge |
| Pain / costly problem | Problem–Agitate–Solution |
| Category / positioning clarity | StoryBrand |
| Sequential case | Pixar Pitch |
| Research / evidence / market insight | Data Story |
| Personal mistake / learning | Confession |

If the idea does not benefit from storytelling:

> **Do not force a story.**

# 12. SAME IDEA, DIFFERENT STORY FRAME

### CORE idea

> **A customer complaint is a signal, not automatically an opportunity.**

### Before–After–Bridge

Before:

> 30 complaints.

After:

> one validated opportunity.

Bridge:

> distinguish signal from opportunity before designing the solution.

### Hero's Journey

Founder sees repeated complaints → assumes product opportunity → investigates → discovers deeper need → reframes opportunity.

### Data Story

Context → rising requests → surprising cluster → underlying operational constraint → strategic implication.

### Confession

> “I used to treat repeated customer requests as validation. I now treat them as evidence that demands investigation.”

Same intellectual idea.

Different cognitive delivery.

- Is the distinction useful?

### Audience

- Is this relevant to the ICP?
- Is it appropriate to the audience state?

### Writing

- Is Insight #1 front-loaded?
- Is Insight #2 genuinely useful?
- Does the payoff arrive while attention is high?
- Is the language clear?

### CORE

- Does it strengthen the CORE worldview?
- Does it sound like CORE rather than generic AI content?
- Does it preserve the free/paid boundary?

### Commercial

- Is the CTA natural?
- Is the next step appropriate?

# 33. EXAMPLE — CUSTOMER REQUEST

### Raw idea

> Customers keep asking for a feature.

### ICP

Founder of a Deep-Tech startup.

### State

Idea.

### Current belief

> “Repeated requests prove demand.”

### Desired belief

> “Repeated requests identify a signal; we still need to understand the underlying job and commercial commitment.”

### CORE idea

> **A customer request is evidence of a problem, not automatically evidence of a product requirement.**

### Insight #1

> “Ten customers asking for the same feature does not necessarily mean you should build it.”

### Insight #2

Break the request into:

> **job → constraint → desired outcome → willingness to change behaviour/pay.**

### Story frame

Before–After–Bridge.

### Channel

LinkedIn short video.

### Payoff

> “The question isn't ‘How many customers asked for this?’ It's ‘What uncertainty does this request actually reduce?’”
