# CORE™ YOUTUBE TEASER CONTENT ARCHITECTURE
## Version 1.0
### Public content designed to create demand for ₹499 and ₹39,999 without giving away the products

---

# 1. PURPOSE

This document defines how YouTube should function as a **teaser and depth channel** for CORE™.

It does not replace the current content architecture.

The existing CORE content architecture says public content should:

> **Reality → Audience Tension → Intellectual Lens → Synthesis → Narrative → CORE Point of View → Conversation → Relationship → Deeper Work / Appropriate Offer.**

It also defines the audience movement as:

> **Interesting idea → This is my problem → I should explore this → I should work through it.**

And the offer-state mapping remains:

> **Signal → ₹499**

> **Idea → ₹39,999**

> **Mature Product / Business → ₹5L**

The YouTube channel should therefore create **recognition, intellectual curiosity and self-diagnosis**, then make the appropriate next step obvious.

It should not become a free version of the paid courses.

---

# 2. WHY YOUTUBE

The current CORE public-discovery architecture identifies LinkedIn as the primary discovery channel.

YouTube should therefore be treated differently:

> **LinkedIn = recognition + conversation**

> **YouTube = deeper demonstration + intellectual trust**

YouTube gives CORE room to show:

- a real business situation;
- a counterintuitive interpretation;
- a worked example;
- a piece of CORE reasoning;
- a partial demonstration.

But it should stop **before delivering the full paid transformation**.

---

# 3. THE JOB OF A YOUTUBE TEASER

A teaser is not:

> “Here are the 7 steps from the course.”

A teaser is:

> **“Here is a business problem you recognize. Here is something you may be seeing incorrectly. Here is a more useful way to think about it. Now you can see why a deeper system would be valuable.”**

The viewer should leave with:

> **a useful realization**

not:

> **a completed CORE workflow.**

---

# 4. THE TEASER PRINCIPLE

## Give away the insight.

## Demonstrate the thinking.

## Withhold the complete operating system.

That means YouTube can reveal:

- distinctions;
- questions;
- examples;
- reasoning moves;
- case fragments;
- test-selection mistakes;
- decision traps.

But it should not provide the entire workbook, full sequence, coaching intervention or complete product implementation.

# 20. THE CTA ARCHITECTURE

The CTA should match the viewer's state.

## Signal-state viewer

Do not say:

> “Buy ₹39,999.”

Instead:

> **“If this made you think about a signal in your own business, start there. CORE Essentials is built to help you turn that signal into a structured opportunity.”**

## Idea-state viewer

Use:

> **“If you already have an idea and the harder question is what you need to test before you commit, that is the work we do in the 30-Day Idea → Market Sprint.”**

## Mature-business viewer

Use:

> **“If the product is already real and the deeper challenge is building the organisation around it, that is a different conversation.”**

This preserves the state-based GTM architecture rather than forcing every viewer through the same funnel.

# 21. WHAT NOT TO DO

The content architecture explicitly warns against:

- generic motivational content;
- generic AI tips;
- book summaries;
- fear-only content;
- framework lectures;
- consultant-style instruction without diagnosis;
- constant product pitches;
- trend-chasing without synthesis;
- engagement bait.

These restrictions apply strongly to YouTube.

Do not create:

> “5 lessons from Roger Martin.”

Create:

> **“A ₹20 Cr industrial opportunity looked attractive. Here's the question I would ask before funding it.”**

The thinking should be experienced, not merely named.

# 26. TEASER QUALITY CHECK

Before publishing, ask:

### Reality

Is there a recognizable business situation?

### Depth

Is there a real intellectual idea?

### Relevance

Does it matter to the intended audience?

### Originality

Is this synthesis rather than repetition?

### Diagnosis

Have we separated symptom from underlying issue?

### Revelation

Does the viewer see something differently?

### Implication

Does it have strategic consequence?

### Continuity

Does it belong to CORE's larger body of thought?

### Restraint

Are we avoiding unsupported claims?

A strong teaser should pass all nine.
