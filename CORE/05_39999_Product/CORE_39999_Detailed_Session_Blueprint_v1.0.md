---

# 1. Document Purpose

This document converts the locked **CORE ₹39,999 Learner Experience Architecture v1.0** into a production-level session specification.

The architecture establishes that the learner experience is:

> **Real Idea/Problem → Problem & Opportunity Framing → Strategic Choice → WWHTBT → Critical Uncertainty → Existing Evidence → Evidence Required → Evidence Selection → Evidence Generation → Interpretation → Decision → Next Commitment.**

The product is explicitly hybrid:

> **online learning + structured work + personalised sessions + evidence generation + decision-making.**

The learner's own problem is the primary working case. The four teaching cases are used as simulations to develop judgment. fileciteturn45file7L847-L871 fileciteturn45file7L955-L968

The five personalised sessions are:

1. Frame
2. Choose
3. Evidence
4. Generate / Learn
5. Decide

The intended learning loop is:

> **Learn → Apply → Submit → Discuss → Revise → Continue**

rather than a watch-only course. fileciteturn45file7L972-L982

---

# 2. Product-Production Principle

Every major lesson should follow the product-building logic already established:

> **Knowledge → Teaching → Experience → Application → Transformation → Evidence → Proof**

And each major lesson should move through:

> **Extract the gold → Define learner transformation → Choose business situation → Build narrative → Teach → Make memorable → Apply → Capture artifact → Capture reflection → Capture proof.** fileciteturn45file0L28-L72

Therefore, this blueprint does **not** treat the five sessions as five lectures.

Each session is a designed experience containing:

- pre-work;
- content;
- case;
- application;
- artifact;
- submission;
- personalised intervention;
- revision;
- gate.

# SESSION 1
# FRAME THE PROBLEM / OPPORTUNITY

## 1. Session Objective

Move the learner from:

> **“I have an idea/problem.”**

to:

> **“I can articulate the problem/opportunity and the decision I am trying to make.”**

The learner must not jump immediately into validation activity.

The first job is to determine what they are actually trying to solve and what decision the work is intended to inform.

---

# 2. Session Outcome

At the end of Session 1, the learner should have:

1. a clear situation statement;
2. a working problem frame;
3. an opportunity frame;
4. a decision statement;
5. alternative interpretations worth keeping alive;
6. a first version of the **CORE Problem & Opportunity Frame**.

---

# 4. Lesson 1.1 — The Difference Between Situation and Problem

## Video objective

Teach the learner to separate:

> **what is happening**

from:

> **what we think is causing it.**

## Central idea

A symptom is not automatically the problem.

## Memorable distinction

> **“What happened” is not the same as “why it happened.”**

## Narrative

### Situation

A company sees repeat orders declining.

### Tension

Management says:

> “Customers are leaving because we are expensive.”

### Challenge

But the evidence may reveal service, reliability, changing requirements or competitive structure.

### Revelation

The initial explanation is a hypothesis.

### Framework

Separate:

> situation → interpretation → problem frame.

### Application

Learner rewrites their own situation without embedding an untested explanation.

# 8. Lesson 1.3 — From Problem to Opportunity

## Objective

Move from:

> “I have a product.”

or:

> “I have a problem.”

to:

> **“There may be an opportunity worth pursuing.”**

The learner should identify:

- who is affected;
- what outcome matters;
- why the problem/opportunity could be strategically relevant.

---

# 9. Lesson 1.4 — The Decision

## Objective

Make the eventual decision explicit.

### Weak

> “I want to validate my product.”

### Stronger

> “Should we pursue this customer segment with this offer?”

or:

> “Should we invest in solving this customer problem?”

The decision must be something evidence can inform.

# 12. Live Session 1 — 45–60 Minutes

## 0–5 min — Context

Ask:

> “What decision brought you here?”

Do not start with:

> “Tell me about your product.”

---

## 5–15 min — Situation

Challenge:

- facts vs interpretation;
- symptoms vs causes;
- assumptions embedded in wording.

---

## 15–30 min — Problem Frame

Ask:

> “What are three different ways this could be framed?”

Then:

> “What would each frame cause you to do?”

---

## 30–40 min — Opportunity

Ask:

> “What makes this worth pursuing?”

---

## 40–50 min — Decision

Ask:

> “What decision would you make if you had enough evidence?”

---

## 50–60 min — Revision

Agree on the revised Problem & Opportunity Frame.

---

- What are you interpreting?
- What are you assuming?
- What else could explain this?

- What decision are you trying to make?
- What would change that decision?

# 17. Lesson 2.1 — Strategic Choice

## Objective

Make the choice explicit.

The learner should be able to state:

> **“We will pursue X, for Y, through Z.”**

where appropriate.

Avoid turning this into a generic strategy lecture.

The learner needs a choice that can later be stress-tested.

---

# 18. Lesson 2.2 — WWHTBT

## Central idea

> **What would have to be true for this choice to work?**

The learner generates conditions rather than predictions.

Possible dimensions include:

- customer;
- problem;
- behaviour;
- buying;
- economics;
- technology;
- integration;
- capability;
- ecosystem;
- regulation/policy.

Not every dimension is mandatory.

---

# 19. Lesson 2.3 — Known vs Assumed

Introduce:

- Known
- Indicated
- Assumed
- Unknown

The purpose is not to label everything.

The purpose is to expose where the strategic choice depends on weak evidence.

---

# 20. Lesson 2.4 — Fragility

Use Case 01 and the startup example.

A company can have:

> working technology

while still having:

> weak commercial adoption evidence.

The learner should ask:

> **“If this condition is false, how badly does the strategy break?”**

# SESSION 3
# CRITICAL UNCERTAINTY + EVIDENCE SELECTION

## 29. Session Objective

This is the central evidence-design session.

Move from:

> **“We have many assumptions.”**

to:

> **“This is the uncertainty that matters most right now, and this is the evidence we need.”**

The architecture explicitly requires the learner to establish the evidence requirement **before choosing a test**. fileciteturn45file8L1030-L1073

---

# 30. Lesson 3.1 — Not All Unknowns Matter Equally

Rank by:

- consequence;
- decision relevance;
- uncertainty;
- learning value;
- time sensitivity.

---

# 31. Lesson 3.2 — Existing Evidence First

Ask:

> **“What do we already know?”**

Inventory:

- internal records;
- prior customer evidence;
- previous tests;
- technical data;
- regulatory information;
- credible external evidence.

Do not create new research simply because:

> “We need validation.”

---

# 32. Lesson 3.3 — Evidence Required

This is a key distinction.

### Weak

> “Interview customers.”

### Strong

> “Evidence that target buyers would take a meaningful commercial step under realistic conditions.”

The learner describes the **evidence**, not the activity.

---

# 33. Lesson 3.4 — Method Selection

Only after defining evidence required does the learner consult the:

> **CORE Evidence-Generation Method Library.**

The four cases demonstrate why there is no universal test:

- Case 01 — commercial commitment;
- Case 02 — internal data + customer evidence + operational triangulation;
- Case 03 — technical test + acceptance criteria;
- Case 04 — authoritative regulatory evidence + expert/customer/commercial evidence. fileciteturn45file8L1077-L1101

---

# 34. Lesson 3.5 — Method Limitations

Every method must be accompanied by:

> **What will this method NOT tell us?**

This prevents overclaiming.

---

# 35. Lesson 3.6 — Acceptance Criteria vs Evidence Standard

### Acceptance criteria

> What constitutes success for the test, where applicable?

### Evidence standard

> How strong must the overall evidence be for this decision?

This distinction is particularly important in Case 03.

# 63. Revision Logic

A revision is not a cosmetic rewrite.

If Session 3 reveals:

> the critical uncertainty was wrong,

then:

> WWHTBT and the evidence plan may need to change.

If Session 4 reveals:

> the evidence contradicts the original problem frame,

then:

> the problem/opportunity frame may need to reopen.

This is a feature, not a failure.

CORE is a learning system.
