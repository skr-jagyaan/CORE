# CORE™ Essentials ₹499
## Graphy Course Production Blueprint
### Version 1.0

---

# 1. Purpose

This document converts the approved **CORE Essentials ₹499 Graphy Product** into a production-ready course blueprint.

It is not another product strategy document.

It specifies:

- what the learner sees
- what the learner watches
- what the learner does
- what artifact the learner creates
- what the learner should understand
- where reflection occurs
- how the course creates the intended transformation
- what should be produced for Graphy

The product transformation remains:

> **Signal → Idea**

More precisely:

> **Signal → structured opportunity → credible test proposal**

The product boundary remains:

> **Prepare the opportunity for deeper testing.**

It does not promise market validation.

---

# 2. Product Experience

The course must feel like a **guided thinking experience**, not a video library.

The core learning loop is:

> **WHY → IDEA → CASE → APPLY → ARTIFACT → REFLECT**

The deeper learner loop is:

> **Understand → Practice → Apply → Reflect → Reinforce**

The customer should repeatedly move from:

> **watching**

to:

> **thinking**

to:

> **doing**

to:

> **saving a useful business artifact**

---

# 3. Final Learner Outcome

At completion, the learner should have:

> **one meaningful business signal converted into a structured, challenged and credible opportunity with a clearly defined next test.**

The learner should be able to say:

> **“I can explain the situation, show the opportunity, compare alternatives, identify what must be true, explain what I know and do not know, and ask for support to run the smallest meaningful test.”**

# 5. Section 1 — THINK ON PAPER

## Module 1 — Why Thinking Needs a System

### Lesson 1.1 — The Problem With Thinking Inside Your Head

#### Format
Video + reflection

#### Duration
7–9 minutes

#### Core idea

Business thinking becomes easier to challenge when it becomes visible.

#### Business situation

Use a familiar example:

> A founder says, “Our customers are asking for this.”

Then separate:

- what was actually observed
- what was inferred
- what is assumed

#### Memory line

> **Thinking becomes easier when you can see it.**

#### Learner task

Take the chosen signal and write:

**What happened?**

**What do I think it means?**

**What am I assuming?**

#### Artifact

**Observation vs Interpretation Sheet**

---

### Lesson 1.2 — Why Structured Notes Matter

#### Format
Video + task

#### Duration
8–10 minutes

#### Teach

Separate:

- situation
- problem
- interpretation
- alternatives
- assumptions
- implications
- action

#### Memory line

> **Do not let observation, interpretation and assumption become one sentence.**

#### Learner task

Rewrite the original signal into:

1. Observation
2. Interpretation
3. Assumption

#### Artifact

**Structured Signal Record**

---

### Lesson 1.3 — Why Decisions Need Records

#### Format
Video + reflection

#### Duration
6–8 minutes

#### Core idea

A decision should be inspectable after it is made.

#### Discuss

A useful decision record preserves:

- what was known
- what was assumed
- what alternatives existed
- why a choice was made
- what evidence was missing

#### Memory line

> **Decisions should survive the person who made them.**

#### Learner task

Record one current business decision using:

> Situation → Choice → Reasoning → Unknowns → Next step

#### Artifact

**Mini Decision Record**

---

## Module 1 Checkpoint

### Learner must have

- My Signal
- Observation vs Interpretation
- Structured Signal Record
- Mini Decision Record

### Checkpoint question

> **What became clearer once you separated what happened from what you think it means?**

# 6. Section 2 — BUSINESS THINKING CANVAS™

## Module 2 — Understand the Canvas

### Lesson 2.1 — Why the Canvas Exists

#### Format
Video

#### Duration
6–8 minutes

#### Core idea

The Business Thinking Canvas™ is a structure for moving from ambiguous business thinking toward:

> **a clearer opportunity + a meaningful next move**

It does not predict success.

It creates inspectable reasoning.

---

## Lesson 2.2 — Strategic Gap

### Question

> **What is changing?**

#### Teach

Identify the gap between:

> **What is**

and:

> **What could be**

#### Case

Use one simple business situation.

#### Learner task

Complete:

- current condition
- desired condition
- what changed
- why the gap matters

#### Artifact

**Canvas v1 — Strategic Gap**

---

## Lesson 2.3 — Strategy-In-Use

### Questions

> What are we doing today?

> What are we treating as true?

> What assumptions are embedded in the current approach?

#### Learner task

Write the current approach and its assumptions.

#### Artifact

**Canvas v2 — Strategy-In-Use**

---

## Lesson 2.4 — Alternatives

### Question

> **What else could we do?**

#### Core idea

The first attractive idea should not automatically win.

#### Learner task

Generate:

> **at least three plausible alternatives**

These can include:

- do more of what we do today
- target a different customer
- solve a different adjacent problem
- change the offer
- change the business model
- do nothing for now

#### Artifact

**Alternatives Map**

---

## Lesson 2.5 — Better Choice

### Question

> **Which alternative deserves deeper examination?**

Important distinction:

> **Better choice ≠ guaranteed choice**

It means:

> the option that, given what we currently know, deserves deeper examination.

#### Learner task

Select one option and explain why.

#### Artifact

**Better Choice Statement**

---

## Lesson 2.6 — What Would Have to Be True

### Question

> **What must be true for this choice to work?**

This is the first bridge to the broader CORE testing architecture.

#### Teach

Possible conditions:

- customer
- problem
- behaviour
- buying
- economics
- technology
- integration
- capability
- ecosystem

At ₹499, do not attempt full validation.

The goal is to make the conditions visible.

#### Learner task

Write:

> **5–10 things that would have to be true.**

#### Artifact

**WWHTBT Starter Map**

---

## Lesson 2.7 — Test

### Question

> **What is the smallest meaningful way to learn?**

#### Teach

A useful next test can be:

- observation
- interview
- customer conversation
- prototype
- pretotype
- small experiment
- landing page
- mock sale
- other proportionate learning action

The test should be linked to the most important unknown.

#### Learner task

Define:

- what we need to learn
- who/what to test
- what action will generate learning
- what result would change our thinking

#### Artifact

**Smallest Meaningful Test**

---

## Lesson 2.8 — Commit

### Question

> **What deserves the next move?**

The commitment can be:

- further discovery
- a conversation
- research
- a prototype
- a small test
- a request for stakeholder support

It does not automatically mean investment.

#### Learner task

Complete:

> **Next Move**

with:

- action
- owner
- timing
- reason

#### Artifact

**Next Commitment Card**

# 19. Final Assessment

There should not be a conventional knowledge-heavy final exam.

The final assessment should be:

> **Can the learner produce a coherent opportunity decision pack from a real signal?**

Minimum criteria:

### Signal clarity

Can they explain what changed?

### Problem clarity

Can they explain what matters?

### Alternative thinking

Did they consider meaningful alternatives?

### Opportunity quality

Can they articulate the opportunity?

### Assumption clarity

Can they identify what must be true?

### Evidence discipline

Do they separate fact from assumption?

### Communication

Can another person understand why the opportunity matters?

### Test quality

Is the next test meaningful and proportionate?

If these are met:

> **The learner has completed the transformation.**

# 20. Production Format for Graphy

Each core learning unit should contain:

### A. Video

Short, focused explanation.

### B. Case / Example

Concrete business situation.

### C. Prompt

A question that forces thinking.

### D. Task

Real application to the learner's signal.

### E. Artifact

A document/template the learner saves.

### F. Reflection

What changed?

Graphy should present these in a consistent visual pattern.

These are **production guidelines**, not hard rules.

### Core concept videos

5–10 minutes

### Case videos

6–12 minutes

### Demonstrations

5–12 minutes

### Task instructions

3–6 minutes

### Reflections

2–5 minutes

The course should err toward:

> **short teaching + real doing**

rather than long lectures.

# 28. Source Boundary

The production blueprint preserves the existing CORE Essentials source architecture:

> **Signal → Canvas → CORE™ GPT → Narrative → Stakeholder → Test**

and the six existing advanced-industry case structures.
