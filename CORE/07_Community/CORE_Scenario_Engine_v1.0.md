# CORE™ Scenario Engine v1.0

**Status:** CURRENT / COMMUNITY INTELLIGENCE MECHANISM  
**Parent:** CORE™ Community Architecture v1.0  
**Purpose:** Define the scenario mechanism that makes Decide With CORE participatory, diagnostic and repeatable.

## 1. Role of the Scenario Engine

The Scenario Engine is a signature community mechanism.

It should turn passive reading into active judgment.

The core pattern is:

> **Situation → Position → New Information → Reconsideration → Evidence → Reflection**

The purpose is not prediction.

The purpose is to explore what different conditions would mean for real choices.

## 2. What a scenario is

A scenario is a bounded situation containing:

- a recognisable context
- a meaningful tension
- incomplete information
- a decision or choice
- realistic constraints
- uncertainty

A good scenario should create a reason to disagree intelligently.

## 3. Scenario types

### Business scenario

A company faces a difficult commercial choice.

### Technology scenario

A technology becomes technically feasible but commercially uncertain.

### Market scenario

Customer behaviour, competition or economics change.

### Industry scenario

A capability, policy or technology alters the industry structure.

### Founder scenario

A founder faces resource, focus, partner, customer or financing choices.

### Organisation scenario

An existing company must change capabilities or management systems.

## 4. Scenario anatomy

Every scenario should define:

```text
Context
↓
What has changed?
↓
What decision is required?
↓
What is known?
↓
What is uncertain?
↓
What constraints exist?
↓
What are the plausible choices?
↓
What would have to be true?
```

## 5. Participation sequence

### Round 1 — Commit

Members make an initial judgement.

### Round 2 — Explain

Members state the reasoning behind the judgement.

### Round 3 — Challenge

Other members challenge assumptions or offer alternatives.

### Round 4 — New evidence

Additional information is introduced.

### Round 5 — Reconsider

Members may revise their position.

### Round 6 — Synthesis

CORE explains the strongest interpretations and remaining uncertainties.

### Round 7 — Learning

The group records:

> what changed the decision?

## 6. Scenario standards

A scenario should be:

- plausible
- specific enough to reason about
- open enough to permit multiple views
- grounded in evidence where facts are asserted
- free from a predetermined “correct” answer unless it is explicitly a case with a known outcome

## 7. Evidence integration

Scenarios can introduce evidence progressively.

Evidence should be distinguished from:

- assumptions
- interpretations
- predictions
- opinions

Use the CORE evidence logic:

> **Decision → Critical Uncertainty → Evidence Required → Evidence Method → Evidence → Interpretation → Decision**

## 8. Scenario outputs

Each completed scenario can produce:

- member viewpoints
- alternative choices
- evidence used
- decision changes
- recurring assumptions
- case notes
- future scenario prompts

## 9. Scenario archive

Each scenario should receive a stable identifier.

Example:

> **SC-001**

Archive fields:

- Scenario ID
- Title
- Date
- Theme
- Industry
- Decision type
- Initial assumptions
- Evidence introduced
- Member positions
- Position changes
- Final synthesis
- Learning
- Related CORE content

## 10. Scenario quality gate

Before publication, check:

- Is the situation understandable?
- Is there a genuine decision?
- Is the uncertainty meaningful?
- Are the constraints realistic?
- Can intelligent people reasonably disagree?
- Is the scenario free from hidden trivia?
- Does new evidence materially change the problem?
- Does the exercise produce learning?

## 11. Public vs member use

Public:

> teaser, situation, partial question.

Member:

> full scenario, discussion, evidence rounds, reconsideration and synthesis.

## 12. Long-term value

The Scenario Engine should become a proprietary library of strategic situations.

Over time it can reveal:

- recurring decision patterns
- common misjudgements
- useful heuristics
- industry-specific uncertainties
- evidence gaps
- emerging opportunities

## 13. Core principle

> **Do not ask members what they think only once. Build situations in which their thinking can change.**
