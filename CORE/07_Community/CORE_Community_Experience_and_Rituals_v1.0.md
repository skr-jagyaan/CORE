# CORE™ Community Experience & Rituals v1.0

**Status:** CURRENT / COMMUNITY EXPERIENCE DOCUMENT  
**Parent:** CORE™ Community Architecture v1.0  
**Purpose:** Define the repeated experiences and rituals that make Decide With CORE a participating community rather than a content library.

## 1. Experience principle

The community must create reasons to return that are stronger than “new content was published.”

The recurring loop is:

> **See → Respond → Compare → Contribute → Apply → Return**

## 2. Member experience

A strong member should progressively feel:

1. **I discover something interesting.**
2. **I have a view.**
3. **Other people see it differently.**
4. **I learned something from the exchange.**
5. **I can use it on a real problem.**
6. **I have something worth contributing next time.**

## 3. Core rituals

### Ritual 1 — The Scenario

A real or plausible situation is presented.

Members are asked to:

- interpret the situation
- take a position
- explain why
- identify what they would need to know

The scenario can evolve as additional information is released.

### Ritual 2 — The Decision Room

A member brings a live problem.

Recommended format:

> Situation → Decision → Critical uncertainty → Evidence → Options → View → Next action

The objective is not to provide consulting to one member while everyone else watches.

The objective is:

> **one problem → shared learning**

### Ritual 3 — The Case Room

A company, industry or decision is examined together.

Members explore:

- what happened
- what people believed
- what evidence existed
- what alternatives were available
- what they would have done

### Ritual 4 — Signal Watch

Members surface something they believe matters:

> a technology shift, customer behaviour, regulation, investment, market movement, capability development or emerging use case.

The community discusses:

> **signal → why it matters → who should care → what to watch next**

## 4. Live rhythm

Initial recommended rhythm:

### Weekly

- one scenario or signal prompt
- member discussion

### Fortnightly

- one deeper live room

### Monthly

- one high-quality member case / decision session
- one synthesis of important member-generated insights

The exact cadence should be tuned after observing participation rather than assumed in advance.

## 5. Member contribution design

Participation should not depend only on long written posts.

Contribution can include:

- a viewpoint
- a question
- a counterexample
- an introduction
- a case
- a source
- a useful tool
- an experience
- a correction
- a changed mind

## 6. Contribution ladder

Members can move through:

> **Observe → React → Explain → Challenge → Contribute → Convene**

The system should recognise useful participation without turning the community into a superficial points game.

## 7. Founder role

The founder should not answer everything.

The founder's role is increasingly to:

- establish the standard
- frame difficult questions
- connect people
- synthesize strong contributions
- introduce evidence
- host selected live rooms
- protect community quality

## 8. Community rituals should create memory

Important discussions should not disappear into a feed.

Create durable memory through:

- Scenario Archive
- Case Archive
- Decision Archive
- Signal Archive
- member insights
- recurring themes
- useful introductions

## 9. Ritual design principles

Avoid:

- engagement bait
- compulsory posting
- shallow daily prompts
- endless polls
- fake gamification
- founder-centred Q&A for every issue

Prefer:

> **interesting question + real stakes + thoughtful participation + useful synthesis**

## 10. Community experience equation

> **Curiosity + Participation + People + Memory + Application = Community Value**

## 11. Desired cultural behaviour

Members should become known for:

- curiosity
- intellectual honesty
- useful contribution
- constructive disagreement
- evidence-sharing
- generous introductions
- willingness to revise a view
- practical application

## 12. Design outcome

The best outcome is not:

> “Members consume a lot.”

It is:

> **“Members keep coming back because interesting people, useful ideas and difficult situations meet here.”**
