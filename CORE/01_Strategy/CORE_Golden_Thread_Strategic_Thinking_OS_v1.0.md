# 3. PROBLEM FRAMING

A problem is not merely a statement of symptoms.

CORE treats problem framing as a strategic act because the framing determines what evidence is relevant and what action is possible.

A usable problem frame contains:

```text
CONTEXT
 + STAKEHOLDER
 + OBSERVED SITUATION
 + DESIRED OUTCOME
 + CONSTRAINTS
 + TIME / DYNAMICS
 + DECISION TO BE MADE
```

### Problem quality gate

A problem is ready for strategic work when:

- the situation is observable;
- the stakeholder and decision owner are clear;
- the decision or outcome matters;
- the problem is actionable;
- contradictions and changing requirements are acknowledged;
- the framing does not smuggle in an untested solution.

---

# 4. STRATEGIC CHOICE

CORE treats strategy as choice rather than aspiration.

Every significant opportunity should make explicit:

- what we will pursue;
- where we will play;
- how we will create value;
- what alternative we are rejecting;
- what must be true for the choice to work.

The output is a consequential choice, not a list of possibilities.

---

# 5. WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE?

For the selected choice, identify the conditions that must hold for the strategy to work.

Each condition should be phrased as a proposition that can be examined.

Example structure:

```text
CHOICE
  ↓
WWHTBT #1
WWHTBT #2
WWHTBT #3
...
```

Prioritise the assumptions that are:

> important to the choice × uncertain × capable of changing the decision

These are the critical uncertainties.

# 6. EVIDENCE SELECTION

CORE does not begin by asking:

> Which test should we run?

It begins with:

> **What decision do we need to make, what is uncertain, and what evidence would credibly change that decision?**

Evidence can come from:

- internal evidence;
- secondary research;
- market / industry research;
- regulatory or policy research;
- expert interpretation;
- customer or buyer conversations;
- observation;
- commercial behaviour;
- technical demonstration;
- controlled experiment;
- prototype;
- field test;
- pilot;
- other credible methods appropriate to the uncertainty.

### Evidence equation

```text
RIGHT DECISION
 × RIGHT UNCERTAINTY
 × RIGHT EVIDENCE
 × RIGHT EVIDENCE METHOD
 = BETTER DECISION
```

A test is one evidence-generation method, not the entire evidence architecture.

---

# 7. EVIDENCE STANDARD

Every evidence effort must specify:

- evidence required;
- source;
- method;
- why the method is credible;
- acceptance criteria;
- decision rule;
- stop condition;
- result;
- interpretation;
- decision changed or confirmed.

The governing principle is:

> **Evidence should change the decision.**

---

# 8. DECISION ARCHITECTURE

Every learning cycle must end in an explicit decision.

Recommended decision states:

```text
GO
PIVOT
PARK
STOP
CONTINUE LEARNING
```

The correct state depends on the evidence and decision rule.

Avoid treating “interesting” or “promising” as decisions.

# 11. DASHBOARD LOGIC

Management visibility should operate at multiple levels.

## Frontline

What decision is being made? What rule applies? What happened?

## Weekly management

What is moving? Where are deviations? Which assumptions are becoming fragile?

## Quarterly finance

What is the economic result? What strategic choices are paying off?

## Cash bleeding

Where is cash being consumed without sufficient evidence of future value?

Dashboards are interfaces to the Golden Thread, not reporting decoration.

# 13. QUALITY GATES

Before moving forward, ask:

### Signal → Idea
Is there a meaningful opportunity rather than merely an interesting observation?

### Idea → Evidence
Is the problem framed and is the critical uncertainty explicit?

### Evidence → Decision
Would this evidence actually change the choice?

### Decision → Organisation
Are capability and decision implications explicit?

### Organisation → Learning
Does the system return information to strategic choice?

# 14. CORE PRINCIPLES

1. Frame the right problem before solving it.
2. Make strategic choice explicit.
3. Start with what would have to be true.
4. Prioritise critical uncertainties.
5. Search existing evidence before generating new evidence.
6. Select the evidence method from the decision requirement.
7. Do not confuse activity with learning.
8. Do not confuse interest with evidence.
9. Make decisions explicit.
10. Translate decisions into organisational capability.
11. Make the thread visible through management systems.
12. Feed learning back into the next decision.
