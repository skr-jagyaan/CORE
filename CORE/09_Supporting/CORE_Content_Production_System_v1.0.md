# CORE Content Production System
## Version 1.0

### Purpose

This document turns the CORE Content Architecture, Narrative Engine and Public Intellectual Engine into a repeatable production process.

It is an operating system for producing public work.

It is not the strategy for what CORE believes.

---

# 1. Production Objective

Produce public work that is:

- grounded in real business situations
- intellectually strong
- relevant to Manufacturing + Deep Tech
- diagnostically clear
- memorable
- connected to the larger CORE body of work
- capable of creating conversation

The goal is:

> **Compounding intellectual memory, not maximum posting volume.**

# 3. Intake Unit

Do not capture:

> “Post idea.”

Capture:

> **Business reality / observation**

Minimum intake:

### Situation

What happened / what is happening?

### Context

What kind of business / leader / problem?

### Tension

What is contradictory or difficult?

### Initial interpretation

What do people normally think is happening?

### Why interesting

Why is the situation worth exploring?

---

# 4. Problem-State Tag

Every idea should be tagged:

### SIGNAL → IDEA

### IDEA → MARKET

### PRODUCT → ORGANISATION

This allows content to remain connected to the Transformation Architecture.

---

# 5. Audience Tension Tag

Select one or more:

- Pain
- Gain
- Curiosity
- Aspiration
- Identity

This prevents content from becoming pain-only.

---

# 6. Intellectual Lens

Ask:

> **What world-class idea can help us see this differently?**

The lens may come from:

- critical thinking
- strategy
- uncertainty
- experimentation
- innovation
- systems
- foresight
- behavioural science

The source is an **angle of attack**, not the topic of the post.

---

# 7. Synthesis

Capture:

> **What does the intellectual lens reveal about the business situation?**

The required output is a new interpretation, distinction or insight.

Example structure:

> Situation + Lens → New understanding

# 10. Drafting Rules

A draft should:

- begin with the business reality
- create tension
- avoid generic motivational language
- use one strong intellectual lens
- contain a clear revelation
- use concrete examples
- avoid over-explaining the framework
- avoid selling CORE in every post
- end with a meaningful question or implication

---

# 11. CORE Brand Visibility

Do not force the brand into every piece.

A post may simply establish the worldview.

CORE should become associated through repetition of ideas such as:

- signal ≠ opportunity
- interest ≠ evidence
- technical feasibility ≠ commercial viability
- find what could kill the opportunity
- test what matters
- evidence changes the decision
- build the organisation the product deserves

---

# 12. Evidence and Claim Control

Before publication, distinguish:

### Observation

What was actually observed?

### Interpretation

What do we think it means?

### External evidence

What is supported by a source?

### Hypothesis

What are we proposing for exploration?

### Claim

What are we asking the audience to believe?

Do not present hypotheses as facts.

---

# 13. Source Discipline

When content derives from a book, research source or external thinker:

- read enough to understand the original idea
- preserve the actual idea
- do not fabricate quotations
- distinguish source idea from CORE synthesis
- cite when the final distribution format requires it

The source is used to deepen the narrative.

The published idea should add your own application and synthesis.

---

# 14. Content Formats

Possible formats:

### Short observation

One strong idea.

### Narrative post

Situation → tension → revelation.

### Case teardown

Situation → reasoning → evidence → decision.

### Essay

Research + synthesis + cases.

### Decision autopsy

What happened → why → what was learned.

### Opportunity signal

A change that may contain a business opportunity.

### Testing note

A critical assumption and how it could be tested.

### Organisational system note

A strategic choice and how it reaches actual decisions.

# 16. Editorial Review

Before publication ask:

### Recognition

Will the intended audience recognize the situation?

### Depth

Is the idea worth the reader's time?

### Diagnosis

Is the underlying issue clear?

### Originality

Have we added synthesis?

### Relevance

Does the idea matter to the audience?

### Narrative

Is there movement?

### Authority

Does the post demonstrate thinking rather than claim expertise?

### Restraint

Are we avoiding unnecessary prescription and hype?

### Continuity

Does it strengthen an existing theme or create a meaningful new one?

---

# 17. Compounding and Reuse

A good piece should be reusable.

One narrative can become:

- short post
- long-form essay
- newsletter
- case teardown
- workshop example
- Opportunity Signal
- sales conversation insight
- CORE principle
- website knowledge asset

The objective is not mechanical repurposing.

It is:

> **Extracting the deeper intellectual value of genuinely useful work.**

---

# 18. Knowledge Archive

Each published piece should be tagged by:

- transformation state
- problem territory
- intellectual lens
- CORE principle
- audience
- format
- evidence source
- related ideas

This creates the transition:

> **Feed → Library → Body of Work**

---

# 19. Conversation Feedback

Comments and conversations should be treated as intelligence.

Capture:

- questions
- objections
- alternative interpretations
- repeated experiences
- new examples
- surprising reactions
- requests for depth

Feed this back into the idea pool.

---

# 20. Network Feedback

Relevant conversations with:

- founders
- manufacturing leaders
- Deep-Tech people
- researchers
- investors
- strategic leaders

should generate new observational material.

The loop is:

> **Network → Conversation → Observation → Content → Public Presence → Network**

---

# 21. Production Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- calendar-driven filler
- posting only because the schedule says so
- summarizing books without synthesis
- generic AI-generated advice
- trend chasing
- repeated product promotion
- repeating the same idea without adding depth
- publishing unsupported claims

---

# 22. Master Production Principle

> **Do not ask what to post. Ask what deserves to become part of the public body of work.**

Then:

> **Observe → Think → Test the idea → Synthesize → Narrate → Publish → Learn → Compound.**
